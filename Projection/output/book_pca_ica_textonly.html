Books: linear projections (PCA and ICA) · variant textonly

A one-off companion to the book map report. UMAP and t-SNE bend the space to keep neighbours together; their axes mean nothing. PCA and ICA are linear: each axis is one fixed direction in the 1,024-dimensional embedding space, so the axes themselves can be read as themes. Data: the 592 book chunks, embedded from their own text only (no chapter path, title or other context line).

What was done

  1. Centring. The average book vector was subtracted from every chunk (both methods need it).
  2. PCA (principal component analysis) finds the directions along which the chunks differ most, in order: PC1 is the single direction with the largest spread, PC2 the largest one at right angles to PC1, and so on. A PCA map shows the chunks' coordinates along two of these directions, at true scale.
  3. ICA (independent component analysis, FastICA) looks for 16 directions whose scores are as statistically independent and as non-Gaussian as possible. In text embeddings such a direction typically picks out a theme: most chunks score near zero, and the chunks about that theme score high. Each component's sign was set so that its long tail is positive. ICA with only 2 components would be a mere rotation of the first two PCA axes, which is why 16 were computed and any two can be plotted.
  4. Reading the axes. For every axis, the chapters with the highest and lowest average score name what it stands for. Chapter labels on the maps sit at the average position of their chunks.

PCA: how much does each direction explain?

Reading it: PC1 explains 4.7% of the differences between chunks, PC2 4.0%, together 8.7%; the first 20 components explain 38%, and 37 components are needed for half. No single direction dominates: even PC1 covers only 4.7%, and the books' content is spread over many dimensions. A 2-dimensional PCA map therefore shows only a thin slice of the structure, which is why non-linear maps (UMAP, t-SNE) are needed for a readable picture.

Cumulative variance:

Reading it: the share of all differences between book chunks explained by the first k principal components together (out of 592 components in total). Two components, the most a flat PCA map can show, explain 9%. Half needs 37 components, 80% 118, 90% 184 and 95% 249. The curve rises gradually: no small set of directions captures most of the content.

Conclusions

  • The main linear direction is largely the difference between the books. PC1 (book η² 0.47) has Anarchoagorismus towards the low end, Anarchokapitalismus towards the high end, but explains only 4.7% of the variation.
  • PCA is a poor map, but an honest one. With no dominant directions, any 2D linear view overlaps most chapters. The compact chapter regions of UMAP and t-SNE come from combining many directions, not from one or two.
  • ICA recovers the books' topics on its own. Without knowing the chapters, its components line up with them: their highest-scoring chapters include “Tři příběhy”, “12. Silnice a dopravní pravidla”, “5. Peníze”, “2. Školství”, “9. Sociální systém”, “Drogy” and more. This is independent, linear evidence that the embedding encodes Urza's ideas as distinct directions.
  • ICA components are candidate “idea axes” for the coverage map. A political question could be scored along each component to see which of Urza's themes it touches. The components depend on the number chosen (16 here) and on the random start, so they are a lens, not a fixed taxonomy.
