Topic distribution · which book concepts do the articles and videos cover? · textonly

The two books are the most systematic statement of Urza's ideas. Here they define the concepts: each book section is a cluster, with a conceptual centre. The 767 article chunks and 980 video chunks are projected into the space the books define and assigned to the nearest centre. The result is how Urza's other output is distributed over the books' concepts, and which texts fit none of them well.

What was done

  1. Embeddings: all chunks, embedded from their own text only (variant textonly). Each source's average vector was subtracted first, so the difference in style between book prose, blog posts and video descriptions doesn't decide the outcome (without this, one section about Urza's organisation “Svobodný přístav” attracted a third of all videos).
  2. The books' concept space: PCA fitted on the 592 book chunks only. Kept are the 75 directions that carry more variance than a random split would (broken-stick rule); they hold 68% of the books' variation. Articles and videos are projected into this space; directions the books don't use are dropped. A typical article or video chunk keeps 0.62 of its length in it.
  3. Conceptual centres: the average of each of the 65 book sections' chunks in this space (chapters; the appendix and AMEN split into their topics; “19. Praxe” split into its subsections).
  4. Assignment: every article and video chunk goes to the centre with the highest cosine similarity. Announcements are left out.
  5. Check on the books: each book chunk was assigned with its own section's centre computed without it. It lands in its own section 66% of the time and among the top three 84% (chance: 1.5%), so the centres are distinct enough to classify by.

How well do articles and videos fit the books at all?

Every text gets a section, even one about something the books never discuss. To see how well texts fit, their similarity to the nearest section centre is compared, in the full 1,024-dimensional space, with how similar the books' own chunks are to their own section (centre computed without the chunk).

Reading it: article and video chunks fit the books' sections less tightly (medians 0.26 and 0.27) than the books fit themselves (0.41). That is expected, since they also contain current affairs, events and people the books don't mention. There is no natural cut-off for “outside the books”: 0% of article and video chunks fit worse than the bottom 5% of book chunks, 12% worse than the bottom 10%, and 60% worse than the bottom quarter. Conclusions

  • The books' concepts cover most of Urza's other output: 58 of 58 topical sections are reached. Articles and videos fit the sections less tightly than the books themselves, and the worst-fitting texts (current affairs, politicians, media, campaigns) mark where Urza goes beyond the books.
  • The distribution is uneven, led by “Ježek bez klece” (5%), “Svobodný přístav” (4%), “8. Školství” (4%), “9. Demokracie” (3%), “12. Anarchie” (3%). The practical, organisational and education topics are far more present in articles and videos than in the books; parts of the economic theory are mainly in the books.
  • Caveats: videos are represented by their descriptions; a chunk gets exactly one section even when it touches several; sections are clusters of the author's own text, so a topic the books treat briefly has a weaker centre. Framing sections (stories, introductions, epilogues) attract texts by tone rather than topic and are shown in italics.
  • For the coverage map: the same centres can score political questions. A question that lands outside every centre, or in a section few texts reach, points to a gap in Urza's coverage.
