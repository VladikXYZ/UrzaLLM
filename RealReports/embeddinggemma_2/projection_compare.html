Raw vs. source-centred embeddings · EmbeddingGemma

Does the embedding place Urza's texts by topic, or by kind of text (book prose, blog post, video description)? For a coverage map we need the first. This page compares the embeddings as they come from the model with a corrected version.

What was done

  1. Embedding. Each of the 2,911 chunks (articles, book passages, video descriptions) was turned into a 768-number vector with the model google/embeddinggemma-2. Texts on similar subjects should get similar vectors (measured by cosine similarity).
  2. Correction (“source-centring”). For each source separately, the average of all its vectors was computed and subtracted from each of its vectors, which were then rescaled to length 1. Whatever all books have in common (style, length) is removed; what makes one book chunk different from another (its topic) stays.
  3. Projection. Both versions were reduced to 2D with UMAP (cosine distance, 15 neighbours, min_dist 0.1, fixed random seed), which keeps nearby texts nearby. The right map was rotated to line up with the left one, so the two are easy to compare. Distances between far-apart clusters on a UMAP map are not meaningful; only who sits next to whom is.
  4. Measurement. For both versions, how often a chunk's nearest neighbours come from the same source (table below).

The two maps

Each dot is one chunk, coloured by source. Hover a dot to see the chunk; the same chunk is circled on the other map. Use the boxes to hide sources and the search to highlight titles (try drug, school or bitcoin).

Raw embeddings

What you see: the map is sorted mainly by kind of text, not by subject: video descriptions (green) gather in one region, articles (blue) and book passages (orange) in another (same-source share of a chunk's 10 nearest neighbours on this map: 71% / 80% / 79% for articles / books / videos, against about 33% by chance). Chunks on the same subject from different sources can end up far apart; search drug to see where the drug chunks land.

After subtracting each source's average

What you see: one main map in which the colours are more mixed than on the left (same-source share of 2D neighbours 71% / 80% / 79% → 68% / 74% / 70%). Some book chapters sit apart as their own islands: Three stories (167 chunks). Search drug again: the matching chunks from different sources stay about as far apart (average distance between them 103% of that on the left). Books still form local groups, because consecutive passages of a chapter really do discuss the same thing.

Same-source share of the 10 nearest neighbours

How it is computed: for every chunk, take the 10 chunks most similar to it and count how many come from the same source (article, book or video); then average over all chunks of that source. Expected at random is what this share would be if similarity ignored the source entirely, i.e. that source's share of all other chunks (books are 33% of the corpus, so a book chunk would have about 3 of 10 book neighbours by chance). The gap between a column and “expected at random” measures how much the source, rather than the topic, decides who is similar. “Full vectors” uses cosine similarity of the 768-number vectors (what the coverage scoring will use); “2D map” uses distance on the maps above.

Reading it: a book chunk's 10 nearest neighbours are 78% books in the raw vectors, against 33% expected by chance. After centring it is 77%. The 2D maps show the same pattern (80% → 74% for books; at most 5 points from the full-vector value for any source), so the maps reflect the full vectors faithfully.

Conclusions

  • The raw embeddings mix topic with kind of text. In the raw vectors every source has far more same-source neighbours than chance would give: articles 73% vs 33%, book passages 78% vs 33%, video descriptions 83% vs 34%. The effect is strongest for video descriptions.
  • Source-centring helps the big picture more than the close neighbourhoods. Measured as the excess over chance, centring removes 2% of it for articles, removes 2% of it for book passages, removes 22% of it for video descriptions (full vectors). Same-source neighbours after centring: 72% / 77% / 72% for articles / books / videos, still well above chance. On the 2D map the share goes from 71% / 80% / 79% to 68% / 74% / 70%, and the separate source regions of the raw map merge into one main map, with same-source patches left inside it. The reason: subtracting an average removes the shift that pushed each source into its own region (hence the more connected map), but the closest neighbours of a chunk are still mostly texts of the same kind.
  • Some of the remaining same-source grouping is genuine. Consecutive passages of one book chapter, a series of lecture announcements or the yearly Rothbard posts really are about the same thing. Nothing is put in front of the chunks (no title, chapter or source name), so this grouping comes from the texts themselves: their style, length and the way each source writes about a topic.
  • For the coverage map, use the centred vectors: that map is organised more by subject than the raw one, which is what “which political issues does Urza cover” needs. Expect sources to still appear as same-colour patches inside each subject area. For scoring a question against chunks, keep the raw vectors: a question has no source, and the model was trained on raw vectors. Because neighbours are still partly same-source, coverage should be reported per source as well as overall.
