Nearest topics in the books · book map, EmbeddingGemma

The simplest possible link structure: on the book map, every topic draws one arrow to the single topic closest to it. Following the arrows, the 56 topics fall into families that end in a pair of topics choosing each other.

What was done

  1. Topics are the 56 chapter groups of the book map (chapters; the appendix and AMEN split into their topics), placed at the average position of their chunks on the same UMAP map. The opening stories were moved closer on the map, as in the book map report.
  2. Nearest in meaning (default): each topic's chunks are averaged into one vector (full 768 dimensions), and each topic points to the topic whose average vector is most similar (cosine).
  3. Nearest on the map (alternative view): the topic whose centre is closest on the 2D map.
  4. Families: following the arrows from any topic always ends in a mutual pair, two topics that are each other's nearest. All topics leading to the same pair form a family.

How to read it: each arrow goes from a topic to its nearest topic; thick double arrows are mutual pairs, where a family ends. Hover a topic for its nearest and next-nearest topics with similarities; click a topic or a family to highlight it. In meaning, there are 6 families; on the map 16. The two views pick the same nearest topic for only 23% of topics: the topic closest in meaning is usually not the one next door on the map. A topic's position is the average of chunks that can be spread out, and UMAP arranges the picture as a whole rather than keeping every pair of close topics side by side. The “in meaning” arrows are therefore the reliable ones; the map gives them a place to be drawn.

Conclusions

  • One greedy step already reveals the books' structure: the families correspond to the books' main themes (economics, law and justice, the state, school, personal freedom).
  • The mutual pairs are the cores of those themes: of the 6 pairs, 1 pair joins a chapter with an AMEN section; 1 pair joins the two books (“Education (Anarchokapitalismus)” ↔ “Education (Anarchoagorismus)”); 4 pairs join other sections of the same book.
  • Limitation: each topic keeps only one link, so a topic close to several themes appears in just one family. The next-nearest topics in the table show the alternatives; the trails report keeps all strong links.
