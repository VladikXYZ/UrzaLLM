Final chunking · Czech and English, aligned

The corpus behind the final reports: Urza's articles, video descriptions and two books, in Czech (embedded) and English (translation, aligned chunk by chunk). One simple rule per source decides what a chunk is.

What was done

  1. Input (preprocessed/): articles with text, all video descriptions, and the two books split into sections, each in Czech and in an English translation of the same items in the same order.
  2. Rules (Chunks/chunk.py): an article's text is one chunk; a video's description is one chunk; in the books, every paragraph (text between blank lines) is one chunk. Nothing is split further or merged. From video descriptions, the recurring boilerplate is removed first: every line found in 20 or more descriptions (donation details, crypto addresses and bank account, Urza's signature line, separators), separately in each language. That was 34% of all video words (median description 142 words before, 88 after); left in, it would make videos resemble each other through the same lines rather than their content.
  3. Alignment: row i of chunks_cs and chunks_en is the same chunk with the same chunk_id (art-0000, vid-0000, AC-033-009 = book, section, paragraph). The script stops if the number of items, sections or paragraphs ever differs.
  4. Metadata from the earlier pipeline: every article and video matches an earlier document by URL, so each chunk keeps its kind (article, announcement, video; 189 announcements), video tags (972 videos), series and guests; video dates come from the channel metadata. Book chapters are stored the same way as before, giving the same 56 chapter groups.
  5. Three embedding texts, as in all earlier reports; only the Czech chunks are embedded (bge-m3), each variant separately:

How long the chunks are

How to read it: share of each source's chunks by number of words (Czech). What it shows: articles (median 146 words) and video descriptions (median 88) are spread widely, from a hashtag to several hundred words; book paragraphs are shorter and more even (median 101, longest 345). Very short book chunks are mostly lines of dialogue in the opening stories and the headings of the money chapter.

Articles and videos over time

How to read it: article (blue) and video (green) chunks per year; one chunk is one article or one video. Books have no year here. What it shows: the change from writing to video over the years, which the timeline report follows in detail.

Czech and English

Lengths: an English chunk has a median of 1.21 × the words of its Czech original (90% between 1.07 and 1.35), as expected for a translation (English uses more, shorter words). Book chunks by section

What it shows: the books split into 56 chapter groups, the same as in the earlier reports. Paragraph chunking changes their weight: the largest are “Three stories” (167), “5. Money” (54), “8. Education” (38), “AMEN: aphorisms” (36), “2. Why does central planning fail?” (32), “19. Practice” (32). The opening stories (“Three stories”, 167 chunks) are dialogue, so nearly every line is its own paragraph; they now weigh much more in the book maps than before. The two AMEN sections (objections and aphorisms) have 128 chunks.

Conclusions

  • The chunks are simple and traceable: one article, one video description or one book paragraph, with the same ID in Czech and English.
  • Articles and videos are as before (video boilerplate removed, as before); the books are now chunked by paragraph, finer than before (976 instead of 592 chunks).
  • The earlier analysis carries over: the same kinds, tags and chapter groups, and the same three embedding variants, so the final reports can be read side by side with the earlier ones.
  • Watch for: the opening stories' many short dialogue chunks, and 22 near-empty chunks.
