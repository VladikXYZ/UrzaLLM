Book idea map · raw embeddings, variant textonly

Do regions of the embedding map correspond to ideas? The two books are the cleanest test: their chapters are topic labels written by the author. This report maps only the book chunks, with the raw embeddings (no source-centring, which is unnecessary when all chunks come from one source).

What was done

  1. Data. The 976 book chunks (each one paragraph of the book, median 122 words) from Anarchokapitalismus and Anarchoagorismus, with their bge-m3 embeddings (variant textonly; text in front of each chunk: nothing: only the chunk's own text).
  2. Chapter groups. Each chunk belongs to one of 56 groups: a chapter, or in the appendix of Anarchokapitalismus its own topic (Drugs, Arms, Science, Work) or AMEN category (Economics, History, …). All AMEN aphorisms of Anarchoagorismus form one group.
  3. Map. UMAP to 2D (cosine distance, 15 neighbours, min_dist 0.1, fixed seed) on these chunks only. Each chapter's name is placed at the average position of its chunks. To avoid a mostly empty picture, the island “Three stories” was moved closer to the rest (its gap shortened about 10×; dashed box on the map). Distances inside the main cloud and inside each island are unchanged; all measurements use the original layout.
  4. Measurements. Who are each chunk's 10 nearest neighbours (full 1,024-dim vectors), and how tightly each chapter's chunks sit together on the map.

A second projection: t-SNE

UMAP is one way to flatten 1,024 dimensions into two. To check that the chapter islands are a property of the embeddings and not of UMAP, the same chunks are projected with t-SNE, a method built on a different principle (it matches neighbour probabilities rather than a neighbourhood graph). Settings: cosine distance, perplexity 30, PCA initialisation, fixed seed; the layout is rotated to line up with the UMAP map. Colours, labels, hover and chapter highlighting work as above and are linked between the two maps.

t-SNE in numbers

Median chapter spread vs random: 0.27 (UMAP 0.17). Chapters below 0.20: 18 of 56 (UMAP 35). Agreement on which chapters are tight or loose (rank correlation of the two columns in the table below): 0.92.

What you see: chapters form islands in the t-SNE map too (18 of 56 below 0.20, against 35 in UMAP), and the two methods agree closely on which chapters are compact and which are spread out (rank correlation 0.92). t-SNE packs chapters less tightly overall (median 0.27 vs 0.17 for UMAP): t-SNE keeps more space inside groups and pulls clusters less apart than UMAP, so the same chapters look looser relative to a random set of chunks. The loosest groups in t-SNE are “17. Self-development”, “19. Practice”, “Myth busting”, “A few words in the introduction”. Because a differently built method shows the same chapter structure, the islands are a property of the embeddings, not an artefact of UMAP. As on the UMAP map, far-away islands were moved closer (dashed box); measurements use the original layout.

Conclusions

  • Yes, regions correspond to ideas, on content alone. Without any label in the text, 35 of 56 chapters still form a compact region (median 6× tighter than random), and the nearest chapters in meaning are the related ones listed under the map. This is the honest evidence that the embedding organises Urza's texts by topic.
  • A spread-out chapter is not a failure of the embedding. Loose groups with few same-chapter neighbours really are mixed in content, the clearest being the broad ones (“19. Practice”, “Myth busting”).
  • The chapter name in the context line inflated the effect, but the content alone still groups chapters. The same chunks embedded with and without the chapter path at the start (chance: 5% same-chapter neighbours, spread 1.00):

  • The books are a good “skeleton” for the full map. Their chapter labels can name the regions of the full map (articles and videos included), since the same topics recur across sources.
