Book idea map · raw embeddings, variant nosource

Do regions of the embedding map correspond to ideas? The two books are the cleanest test: their chapters are topic labels written by the author. This report maps only the book chunks, with the raw embeddings (no source-centring, which is unnecessary when all chunks come from one source).

What was done

  1. Data. The 976 book chunks (each one paragraph of the book, median 122 words) from Anarchokapitalismus and Anarchoagorismus, with their bge-m3 embeddings (variant nosource; text in front of each chunk: “Anarchokapitalismus › 10. Zdravotnictví”).
  2. Chapter groups. Each chunk belongs to one of 56 groups: a chapter, or in the appendix of Anarchokapitalismus its own topic (Drugs, Arms, Science, Work) or AMEN category (Economics, History, …). All AMEN aphorisms of Anarchoagorismus form one group.
  3. Map. UMAP to 2D (cosine distance, 15 neighbours, min_dist 0.1, fixed seed) on these chunks only. Each chapter's name is placed at the average position of its chunks. To avoid a mostly empty picture, 3 separate islands of “Three stories” were moved closer to the rest (gaps shortened about 9–11×; dashed boxes on the map); the island “6. Firefighters” was moved closer to the rest (its gap shortened about 3×; dashed box on the map). Distances inside the main cloud and inside each island are unchanged; all measurements use the original layout.
  4. Measurements. Who are each chunk's 10 nearest neighbours (full 1,024-dim vectors), and how tightly each chapter's chunks sit together on the map.

A second projection: t-SNE

UMAP is one way to flatten 1,024 dimensions into two. To check that the chapter islands are a property of the embeddings and not of UMAP, the same chunks are projected with t-SNE, a method built on a different principle (it matches neighbour probabilities rather than a neighbourhood graph). Settings: cosine distance, perplexity 30, PCA initialisation, fixed seed; the layout is rotated to line up with the UMAP map. Colours, labels, hover and chapter highlighting work as above and are linked between the two maps.

t-SNE in numbers

Median chapter spread vs random: 0.10 (UMAP 0.04). Chapters below 0.20: 45 of 56 (UMAP 49). Agreement on which chapters are tight or loose (rank correlation of the two columns in the table below): 0.82.

What you see: chapters form islands in the t-SNE map too (45 of 56 below 0.20, against 49 in UMAP), and the two methods agree closely on which chapters are compact and which are spread out (rank correlation 0.82). t-SNE packs chapters less tightly overall (median 0.10 vs 0.04 for UMAP): t-SNE keeps more space inside groups and pulls clusters less apart than UMAP, so the same chapters look looser relative to a random set of chunks. The loosest groups in t-SNE are “19. Practice”, “Conclusion; and what next?”, “Three stories”, “Myth busting”. Because a differently built method shows the same chapter structure, the islands are a property of the embeddings, not an artefact of UMAP. As on the UMAP map, far-away islands were moved closer (dashed box); measurements use the original layout.

Reading it: 73% of a chunk's nearest neighbours are from its own chapter, against 5% by chance. Part of that is trivial: 64% are from the very same section (neighbouring paragraphs of the same text). Looking only at neighbours from other sections, 26% are still from the same chapter group, i.e. different parts of a chapter find each other. 95% of neighbours are from the same book (56% by chance): the two books differ in subject and tone.

How tight is each chapter?

Reading it: the median chapter has a spread ratio of 0.04, i.e. its chunks are about 24× more concentrated than random; 49 of 56 groups are below 0.20. The loosest groups (0.30 or more) are: Three stories (1.25), 19. Practice (0.65), AMEN: Economics (0.41), Preface (0.33), 6. Firefighters (0.32). 2 of them also have few neighbours from their own chapter (at most 42%), so their chunks really are scattered: “19. Practice” is 9 different subsections (Hedgehog without a cage, Free port, Cryptoanarchy, Agora, …), so it is spread because the content is. Others are loose for a different reason: their chunks still find each other, but the map places them apart. “Three stories” (100% same-chapter neighbours) lies in 3 separate clumps (one per subsection); “AMEN: Economics” (67% same-chapter neighbours) lies in 3 separate clumps; “6. Firefighters” (82% same-chapter neighbours) has 11 of its 12 chunks in one clump and the other one elsewhere.

Conclusions

  • Regions of the map correspond to ideas. 49 of 56 chapters form a compact region and related chapters lie next to each other; see the caveat below about the chapter name in the text.
  • A spread-out chapter is not a failure of the embedding. Loose groups with few same-chapter neighbours really are mixed in content, the clearest being the broad ones (“19. Practice”). “Three stories”, “AMEN: Economics”, “6. Firefighters” keep mostly same-chapter neighbours and are loose only because the map splits them into separate clumps.
  • The chapter name in the context line inflated the effect, but the content alone still groups chapters. The same chunks embedded with and without the chapter path at the start (chance: 5% same-chapter neighbours, spread 1.00):

  • The books are a good “skeleton” for the full map. Their chapter labels can name the regions of the full map (articles and videos included), since the same topics recur across sources.
